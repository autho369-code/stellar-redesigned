# Competitive research — community association management, August 2026

> Research across the national firms, the board buyer journey, and 710 board
> posts. Everything below is sourced. Where a figure could not be verified it
> says so. This exists so the next person building on this site starts from
> evidence rather than from instinct.

## The four findings that matter

**1. Nobody in this category has an interactive tool.**
Audited across FirstService Residential, Associa, RealManage, CCMC, The
Management Trust and Sentry — roughly 500 pages. Zero calculators, zero
scorecards, zero self-assessments, zero RFP templates, zero transition
checklists. Site searches for "calculator" return nothing on every domain. The
two things that look like tools are not: RealManage's "HOA Health Check" is its
standard proposal form under a different heading, reusing the same HubSpot form
ID as `/request-a-proposal/`; CCMC's "free lifestyle assessment" is a heading
over a contact form.
→ Shipped: `/tools/compare-management-proposals`.

**2. Nobody publishes pricing.** Not a fee, not a range, not a per-door figure.
Sentry's contract-transparency pledge — "You will never be surprised by an
additional charge that is not revealed in the contract" — is the only
substantive engagement with cost across all six. Stellar already publishes.

**3. "No better option" is the real obstacle to switching, not ignorance.**
Boards diagnose the problem precisely and then conclude the market has nowhere
better. This is the single most important positioning fact in the research.
Any pitch aimed at these boards has to beat *resignation*, not a competitor.

**4. The gaps nobody fills**, in order of how defensible each would be:
portfolio cap per manager · line-item fee schedule · firm-level credential and
licence verification · sample work product · contract terms stated publicly.
Stellar now leads on the third.

## What boards actually complain about

Frequency across 305 board-perspective posts mentioning their management
company (r/HOA, r/fuckHOA, r/HOA_Board_Members, via the Arctic Shift archive —
Reddit blocks direct crawling). Counts are floors, not ceilings.

| Theme | % of posts |
|---|---|
| Unresponsive, ignored, no reply | 23.6 |
| Actively firing or shopping | 15.4 |
| Records / financial access blocked | 12.8 |
| Fees, "not worth it" | 5.9 |
| Manager keeps changing | 4.9 |
| Self-management as escape hatch | 4.6 |
| Acquired / PE roll-up / "went corporate" | 4.3 |
| Contract lock-in, auto-renew, termination fee | 3.9 |
| Financials late or wrong | 3.0 |
| Too many communities per manager | 2.0 |
| Vendor kickbacks, markups, no bids | 1.3 |

The bottom four are under-counted relative to their salience — they surface in
comments rather than post bodies, and that is where the heat is. Contract terms
and vendor self-dealing are what boards discover *after* they are stuck.

Corroborating survey data — AppFolio/HOA-USA, March 2022, sample size not
disclosed, treat as directional: unresponsiveness 66%, follow-through failure
65%, poor customer service 61%. **Only 23% would recommend their current
management company.** Price is not in the top three.

Buildium/CAI 2024 (536 board members): 27% say finding and working with a
manager is a top source of stress; 70% of management companies planned to raise
fees; 90% planned to grow by acquisition. An Atlanta firm on its competitors:
"Consolidation by the larger companies is backfiring. Their service level drops
every time they acquire someone, so we will keep picking up their clients."

**No published survey of board satisfaction with management companies exists.**
CAI's flagship instrument surveys *residents* about managers, not boards about
firms. That gap is itself a finding.

## Market context (Foundation for Community Association Research, 2025)

373,000 US associations · 60,000–65,000 managers · 9,000–10,000 management
companies · 30–40% of associations self-managed.

**Illinois is the 4th largest community association market in the country** —
19,850 associations, 1,755,000 units, 4,421,000 residents. Behind only
California, Florida and Texas; ahead of New York, Colorado and Arizona.

Derived, and defensible because the denominators are CAI's own: with 30–40%
self-managed, roughly 224,000–261,000 associations are professionally managed
across 60,000–65,000 managers — about **3.5 to 4.4 associations per manager**
population-wide. That average includes on-site managers, who are 1:1 by
definition, which is what pulls it down. The useful framing: a portfolio
manager carrying 15–20 associations carries four to five times the national
average load.

Published caps and red lines: "more than 15 to 18 associations is typically
overextended" (AMCC); "no more than 15–20 communities" (Gordon James); a board
comparing firms in Michigan found their old firm averaged 20 communities per
manager and "most of the other companies we checked out averaged 10."

Attribution required by the source: "Reprinted with permission of the
Foundation for Community Association Research."

## Credentials — verified against CAI's live directory, 2026-08-22

| Credential | Level | Active records |
|---|---|---|
| CMCA | individual | 25,000+ certified to date (CAMICB) |
| AMS | individual | 5,412 |
| PCAM | individual | 1,923 |
| RS | business partner | 362 |
| **AAMC** | **firm** | **228** |
| CIRMS | business partner | 129 |
| LSM | individual | 91 |

**AAMC is the only firm-level credential** — roughly 2.3% of US management
companies, about 1 in 43. None of the six firms audited holds it. Sentry holds
AMO from IREM, a different body, and builds a page around it.

CMCA is issued by CAMICB, which is independent of CAI, and is the only
credential in this field accredited by the NCCA and to ISO/IEC 17024.

Phrase counts as directory listings, not a census — holders can delist.

**AAMC requirements**, if ever pursued: three years continuous operation, at
least three full-time employees, 50% of qualifying managers credentialed, a
PCAM as senior manager *employed by the firm*, monthly client account
reconciliation, $100,000 fidelity bond plus one of four additional protections,
quarterly financials to every client, six core services to no fewer than three
associations, and **a different PCAM supervisor in every branch office**. That
last rule is the sharpest competitive fact in the set: an AAMC firm cannot
scale by acquisition without a PCAM in each office.

## The buyer journey

**Who decides:** the board, not the membership. In Illinois, 765 ILCS 605/18.4
gives the board the power to engage a managing agent — no owner vote. Two votes
are needed: terminate the incumbent, and hire the replacement.

**Timeline:** 90–180 days end to end. Notice periods of 30, 60 or 90 days are
all common, and auto-renewal is the trap — one real contract required notice
"not more than 180 days and not less than 90 days" before expiry.

**Seasonality is mechanical.** Working back from a 1 January handover with a
90–180 day search, the decision conversation happens **July–September** and
notice is served late October. December outreach reaches boards that have
already auto-renewed.

**Triggers:** developer turnover (Illinois requires turnover within 60 days of
conveying 75% of units, or 3 years from recording the declaration, whichever
comes first); self-management breaking down; service failure; fee increase at
renewal; a crisis such as an insurance shock or a failed reserve study.

**How boards find candidates**, in descending order of trust: referrals from
other boards → the association attorney → CAI directories → search.

## What competitors do well, and what to take

**FirstService Residential** — four-item audience-named nav; 470 corporate
articles across 27 categories plus ~2,500 regional; a 121-item resource library
filtered by topic *and* state; 78 on-demand webinars; ~134 city pages that name
adjacent submarkets and publish the local office address; operational proof
numbers rather than vanity stats ("over 100,000 calls per month", "answered in
40 seconds or less"); **HODA**, a trademarked AI SMS concierge for residents;
and **BENCHMARK**, a proprietary operating-cost report built from their own
~4,000-building portfolio. Notably, they have *stripped all corporate scale
numbers* from their marketing since 2013.

**Associa** — a 3,420-word HTML **AI info page** written explicitly for LLMs,
footer-linked and dated, with source-attribution lines; **134 downloadable
templates and worksheets** rather than essays (proxy forms, inspection
checklists, goal-setting worksheets); a sequenced six-course board academy;
by-role content filtering (Homeowner / Board Member / Employee); named product
sub-brands. Neither Associa nor FirstService has an `llms.txt` — both 404.

**Sentry** — the only firm naming "Education" in primary nav; the best-qualified
lead form of the six, capturing unit count and on-site intent with a "Don't
Know" option; AMO accreditation given its own page; and the contract
transparency pledge.

**CCMC** — the only real LMS in the category (TutorLMS, 10 modules, quiz,
certificate) though it is aimed at land developers and hidden from the nav; and
lifestyle programming treated as a strategic service line rather than an
add-on.

**Their mistakes, worth not repeating:** FirstService's 11-required-field RFP
wall; Associa's proposal form with unconfigured Webflow placeholder dropdowns
("First choice / Second choice") on its highest-value question, no contact
fields at all, an empty reviews page and a location finder containing no
finder; RealManage shipping literal "Test Webinar Signup Page" and "Test Guide
Page" placeholders to production; The Management Trust putting a math captcha
on a B2B proposal form.

## Verified statutory facts used on the site

- **765 ILCS 605/22.1**, as amended effective 2023-01-01: response within **10
  business days**; fee capped at **$375**, adjusted annually by CPI; up to
  **$100** additional for completion within 72 hours. Condominiums only —
  CICAA governs many HOAs and townhome associations.
- **Illinois CAM licensing** (225 ILCS 427): individual licence, 20-hour
  pre-licensing course, exam, renewal 31 August of odd-numbered years, 12 hours
  CE every two years. Exemption for associations of 10 units or fewer. Since
  **2 June 2023** every CAM *firm* must also be licensed and must designate a
  single licensed leader, the **DCAM**.
- **Chicago Exterior Wall Program**: buildings 80 feet and taller, ongoing
  visual inspections plus a critical examination every 4–12 years depending on
  construction type, filed with the Department of Buildings; report classifies
  the building safe / safe with a repair programme / unsafe.
- **Chicago heat ordinance**: 15 September – 1 June, 68°F 8:30am–10:30pm and
  66°F overnight where residents do not control their own heat; penalties to
  $1,000 per day per violation.

## Still open — needs owner input, cannot be inferred

These are the remaining gaps nobody in the category fills. Each needs a real
number from Mirsad; none can be written without one.

1. **Portfolio cap per manager.** The strongest remaining differentiator in the
   entire research set. Nobody publishes one. Pair the number with the CAI
   arithmetic above.
2. **Line-item fee schedule** covering the ancillary categories boards discover
   late: extra board meetings, after-hours calls, resale and transfer
   documents, collection administration, special-assessment administration,
   project oversight, portal fees, copying and postage, transition and exit
   fees.
3. **Contract terms in public**: notice period, whether the agreement
   auto-renews, termination cost, and whether records are returned at no cost.
   "Everything should be turned over as it is the property of the client and
   not the management firm" is only said aloud because it is contested.
4. **Vendor policy.** Whether Stellar marks up vendor work or receives any
   commission or referral fee. On kickbacks, the industry observation is that
   "the red flag isn't suspicious language — it's the absence of language."
5. **Sample work product** — a real manager's report, board packet and monthly
   financial statement, anonymised. Boards ask for all three in RFPs.
6. **Small-association floor.** Multiple boards report firms refusing under-50
   communities or capping "small" at 25 units. Stellar has a small-association
   page; if there is a real floor or minimum fee, saying so serves boards
   better than silence.
7. **Response-time standard.** Note this is *table stakes*, not a
   differentiator — Ardent Residential publishes "if we don't return your calls
   or emails within 2 hours, fire us immediately." Match the field, do not lead
   with it.
