> *business* brand SERP. This one is about the query **"Mirsad Cerimovic"** and
> the arrest-blotter result on page one.

## The target, verified 2026-08-21

**`patch.com/illinois/elmhurst/6-elmhurst-duis-over-weekend-cops`**
Elmhurst Patch · published 7 March 2022 · by Andrea Earnest, Patch Staff

It is a **police-blotter roundup of six arrests**. The name appears once, in a
list, alongside five other people. It reports charges — DUI, concealed firearm
under the influence, improper turn, improper lane usage — and that the person
was released on bond. **It records an arrest, not a conviction, and it states
no case outcome.**

### Why that shape is favorable

A dedicated article about a person is nearly immovable. A four-year-old list
post where the name appears once is not:

- The page is **not about him** — it is not titled, headed, or optimized for the
  name. It ranks on Patch's domain authority and on the absence of competition,
  not on relevance.
- It is **aging**, and blotter roundups accumulate no links or engagement over
  time.
- Because it names six people, an editor can **strike one name** without
  unpublishing the article — a much smaller ask than a takedown.

> A caution on process: the first search result that surfaced during this audit
> pointed at a *different* Patch article and came with a confident summary
> attached. Fetching it showed it was about an unrelated person. Verify any URL
> before acting on it — including anything a vendor sends you.

## 1. Removal at the source (highest leverage by far)

Getting the name off the page beats years of suppression, because it also
clears every scraper and aggregator that copied it.

**Contact Patch.** Their Illinois regional editor, or the contact route on the
Elmhurst Patch page. The ask is specific and small: remove one name from a
four-year-old roundup, or append the case outcome.

This is materially stronger if the charges were **dismissed, reduced, resolved
without conviction, or completed under supervision** — attach the disposition.
Many local outlets now run "clean slate" or right-to-be-forgotten policies for
exactly this situation.

> Subject: Request to update a 2022 Elmhurst blotter item
>
> Hello,
>
> I'm writing about "6 Elmhurst DUIs Over Weekend: Cops," published 7 March
> 2022 on Elmhurst Patch. My name appears in the list of arrests.
>
> The item reports an arrest from four years ago and carries no case outcome. I
> have attached the court disposition showing [OUTCOME].
>
> I'd like to ask that you either remove my name from the list or add a line
> noting the outcome. I'm not asking you to unpublish the article — the other
> five names and the reporting itself are unaffected either way.
>
> The item is currently the first thing that appears when anyone searches my
> name. I run a licensed community association management firm in Chicago and
> the association boards I work with search me before hiring us.
>
> I'm happy to provide any documentation that helps.
>
> Thank you for considering it,
> Mirsad Cerimovic
> mirsad@stellarpropertygroup.com · 773.728.0652

**Also worth a licensed opinion:** ask an Illinois criminal-records attorney
whether this arrest is eligible for expungement or sealing. If it is, the
request stops being a favor and starts being a demand with a court order behind
it. Illinois treats DUI dispositions differently from most offenses — including
supervision — so this needs a real lawyer's answer, not a general one.

**Google's own routes** are narrow but free to try. "Results about you" handles
personal contact information rather than news reporting. Google also demotes
sites engaged in *exploitative removal practices* — if any mugshot aggregator
has copied this item and charges for removal, report it under that policy
rather than paying.

## 4. Expectations, honestly

**Months, not weeks.** Patch is an authoritative domain and the page has four
years of age. The profile page went live today and has no history yet. Expect
first movement in 6–10 weeks and a realistic page-one displacement in 4–6
months, assuming the profile list above actually gets built.

**The order things happen:** the profile page and LinkedIn stabilize first,
then each new profile displaces one more result, then the Patch item drifts to
positions 8–10 before falling off. It does not vanish; it sinks.

**Removal at the source can collapse that timeline to days.** Which is why §1
is first.

## What not to do

- **No reputation-repair vendors** promising removal. They cannot remove a news
  article. The ones that build fake profiles and spun blog networks get the
  whole cluster deindexed, which leaves you measurably worse off than today.
- **No fake profiles or personas.** Beyond the ethics, Google's entity matching
  is good enough that inconsistent fakes actively dilute the real entity.
- **No paying a mugshot aggregator.** Paying confirms the address is live and
  invites the next one. Report them under Google's exploitative-removal policy.
- **No pressuring Patch or its reporter.** Ask once, politely, with
  documentation. Escalation against a news outlet is how a four-year-old list
  post becomes a new article.
